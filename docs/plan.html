Wordbank

A pocket vocabulary trainer for general English. You learn a word on a card, prove you remember it, then use it in your own sentences until it's yours. Built for one person, 30 minutes a day, on a phone. British English throughout.

How a word moves

Four stages, from new to used

Every word carries a status. It only moves forward when you show you know it, so the app always knows what to give you next.

Stage 1

New

You see the word on a card and tap to reveal meaning, sound, examples.

→ tap "I know this"
Stage 2

Learning

The card comes back in later sessions until you recall it before flipping.

→ recall it 2 times in a row
Stage 3

Remembered

Now it turns up in fill-the-gap and pick-the-word exercises.

→ 3 correct answers
Stage 4

Applied

You've rewritten plain sentences with it. Comes back for a check-up after 1, 3, then 7 days.

✓ yours

Levels

Three levels, 500 words

Words are grouped by level only. Each word also carries a source tag so you can filter, for example "Advanced, GRE only", but the main path is Basic → Intermediate → Advanced.

Basic · ~150Common words worth using correctly and confidentlyeager · reluctant · ensure · vital
Intermediate · ~200Sharper, more precise everyday and work wordsconcise · mitigate · candid · prioritise
Advanced · ~150Exam-level and boardroom-level wordsmeticulous · pragmatic · obfuscate · laconic

Source tags

General · ~160Everyday English for conversation, writing, readinghassle · dreadful · thorough

Executive & strategy · ~100Polished: words that sound senior (articulate, delegate, cadence). Strategy: how leaders talk about markets and bets (moat, flywheel, unit economics, optionality). Jargon decoder: meeting phrases with what they really mean and a plain alternative (circle back, bandwidth, table stakes).headwinds · go-to-market · north star

For these, the "everyday" example on the card becomes a plain-English analogy, so you can explain the term, not just recognise it.cache · observability · latency · idempotent · deprecate · inference · fine-tuning · hallucination · guardrails
Example Tech & AI card · Intermediate
observability /əbˌzɜː.vəˈbɪl.ə.ti/ · noun

How well you can tell what's happening inside a system from the data it gives out, such as logs, metrics and traces.

Analogy A car dashboard with good gauges and warning lights has high observability; one with no dashboard leaves you guessing.

At work "Before we scale this service, we need better observability so we can spot slow requests early."

Related monitoring, telemetry, visibility · Opposite black box

Keeping it fresh

The word bank updates itself

The words live in the app's own database, separate from the page, so new words can be added without rebuilding anything.

  • Weekly top-up. A scheduled Claude task runs once a week (say Sunday evening) and adds about 20 new words: a mix of levels and tags, in the same card format, checked against what's already there so nothing repeats.
  • "New this week" shelf on the Home screen shows what arrived. They start at stage 1 like any other word.
  • Balanced intake. The task looks at your progress and leans towards the level you're working on, so you're not flooded with Advanced words while still on Basic.
  • On request. You can also ask Claude any time: "add 30 more AI words".

The first 500 are written in batches of 50 during the build. Your progress stays private to your account.

Scope

What's in version 1

In

  • 500 words across 3 levels, tagged General, GRE / GMAT, Executive & strategy, or Tech & AI
  • Weekly automatic top-up of ~20 new words
  • Flip cards with British pronunciation audio (phone's built-in voice)
  • Four-stage progress per word, with spaced check-ups
  • Fill the gap and pick-the-word practice, generated from your words
  • Rewrite exercises checked against a model answer
  • Daily 30-min session, quick 5-min mode, streak
  • Progress saved to your account, so phone and laptop stay in sync

Later, if useful

  • Add your own word and have the card filled in for you
  • GRE-style "pick two words that give the same meaning"
  • Listening mode: hear a sentence, type the missing word
  • Speaking practice with the microphone
  • Reminder notifications (not possible inside this kind of page)
  • App-store app, accounts for other people, sharing

Build plan

Six steps

  1. Word bank.Set up the database, then write the 500 cards in batches of 50, each with a gap-fill sentence and a rewrite with model answer.
  2. Learn mode.Card deck, flip, audio, "Still learning / I knew it", stage tracking.
  3. Practise mode.Fill the gap and pick-the-word, with distractors drawn from the same bucket.
  4. Apply mode.Rewrite prompts, feedback, model answers.
  5. Daily session and progress.Session builder, check-up schedule, streak, word list. Then test it on a phone.
  6. Weekly top-up.Set up the scheduled task that adds new words (with your OK).
